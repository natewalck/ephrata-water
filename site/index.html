What was tested, and what they found

Lead is measured at household taps every few years. EPA’s “action level” is the point where a utility must take corrective action, and results are reported as the 90th percentile of the samples collected.

PFAS and other unregulated contaminants

In 2024–2025, EPA required most water systems to test for 29 PFAS (“forever chemicals”) and lithium. These are the measured results for this system’s treated water.

Violations on record, in plain English

EPA keeps roughly ten years of history. A “violation” is any missed requirement — most are paperwork (a late test or report), not unsafe water. Health-based violations, where a measured level actually exceeded a legal limit, are marked distinctly.

    What the public can and cannot see

    The only measured results published in machine-readable federal data are lead and copper (every few years) and the 2024–25 PFAS survey above. Routine results for everything else — bacteria, disinfection byproducts, nitrates and more — are reported to regulators but not published anywhere a program can read: Pennsylvania’s open-data feed of sample results has not been updated since March 2021, and the authority’s own reports are not machine-readable.

    About this data

    Ephrata Borough’s tap water comes from the Ephrata Area Joint Authority (system ID PA7360045), drawn roughly half from Cocalico Creek and half from groundwater wells.
